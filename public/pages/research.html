Innovation

Here are a few of the innovative solutions we have developed over the last years ...


Biometrics for multi-factor Authentication

Biometrics-based user data and profiles are easily copied, and thus, for user authentication purposes, should ideally be used as a secondary authentication method in a multi-factor user authentication process, and should always be read and verified within a secure environment.

We designed, prototyped and evaluated innovative and secure biometric authentication for both biometric-based debit/credit transactions and digital-signatures, using match-on-card technology, using biometrics as a means to access and unlock primary cryptographic authentication processes.

Distributed Debit Authorization using CDA

CDA (Combined Data Authentication) is an EMV-defined method for debit/credit card authentication, normally processed locally by POS terminals.

On behalf of a Brazilian bank, Nascent designed and implemented the innovative use of CDA authentication between ATM terminals and a distributed network consisting of hundreds of branch servers, thus eliminating the need to upgrade these servers with expensive cryptographic hardware and enhanced communications.

Smartcard-based MITB countermeasures

A new class of threats called MITB (man-in-the-browser) attacks against internet-banking was discovered in 2008, in which malicious code is injected and directly executed within the browser environment of the users computer.

We designed an innovative asymetric-key based mutual authentication process, in which the smartcard dynamically authenticates both the banking host and transaction data before digitally signing the transaction, and went on to coach the customers development team during the project design, implementation, validation and deployment phases.

GlobalPlatform for MULTOS

Nascent created an innovative solution for a customer who wished to combine the security of the MULTOS smart-card platform and the remote card-management and application provisioning capabilities of the GlobalPlatform standard.

Nascent specified, prototyped, implemented and verified an Issuer Secure Domain application for MULTOS, which implements a card-wide global-PIN, as well as permitting card-resident applications to use standard Secure Channel Protocols and secure delegated card management functions.

Profile-based Digital-Signature Validation

In a corporate environment, the use of digital-signatures for user authentication, document signing and administrative workflows and approvals, device authentication and inter-company secure communication rapidly increasing. A major problem that companies are facing is how to validate all these different digital signatures and certificates, since different business processes demand different validation rules.

Nascent has developed techniques for profile-based signature and certificate validation, in which management of distinct profiles and rules is centralized, but the validation processes are distributed between multiple servers, sites and devices.

Research

As a company, we dedicate a certain amount of time and resources towards research, investigating and contributing new ideas and solutions for specific problems. We strongly believe that the following area is one of the greatest technological challenges we current face as a society.


Pervasive Security - Ubiquitous Cryptographic Technology

As we continue to migrate towards an fully interconnected online digital society, impulsioned not only by convergence and the widespread adoption of end-user devices such as smartphones and tablets, but also by the cloud-based virtualization of services and by pervasive-computing or "the Internet of Things", the underlying security processes, or more specifically, the methods of identification, authentication, authorization and permission-verification become more and more critical.

Cryptograpy is currently the only recognised means of guaranteeing strong remote authentication and authorization, a prerequisite for any form of secure computing. However, use of cryptography and Cryptographic Technology by itself is insufficient, since mobile, embedded and even virtualized operating-systems are currently too fragile to protect the critical assets, such as cryptographic keys, and secure processes, that are required to guarantee overall security.

Evidently, there is an urgent need to rethink user, device and process authentication mechanisms. A major advance in this area has been the creation and adoption of open standards for robust, protected cryptographic methods and storage, as seen with secure-cryptoprocessor style technologies, including smart-cards, SIM cards, secure tokens and micro-SD cards, NFC secure elements and, to some extent, the infamous TPM.

We believe that an important next step will be the transparent and seamless integration of these Trusted Execution Environments into microprocessors, virtualization hypervisors and operating systems, which will open the doors to a wide range of new techniques such as strong cryptographic micro-authentication between privileged and non-privileged processes or code sections, along with efficient and secure cryptographic-based permissions and credentials.

These steps, together with the crucial work being done on formally-verified correctness for micro-kernels and hypervisors, application sandboxing, lightweight virtual machines, and the myriad additional ideas and advances being proposed and undertaken in the domain of secure computing will pave the way for the next generation of truly-secure devices and systems.
